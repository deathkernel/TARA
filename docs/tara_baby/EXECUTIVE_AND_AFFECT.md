# Affect and Executive Control

## Research grounding

Contemporary emotion research emphasizes that emotional perception and response depend on both bottom-up stimulus properties and top-down context and expectations. Appraisal frameworks likewise treat relevance, goal congruence, control and related evaluations as important variables in emotional responses. citeturn142070search7turn142070search10

Goal-directed behavior is studied as an interaction among prefrontal and striatal systems, with explicit goals, strategies and action values. A 2026 perspective emphasizes hierarchical engagement with goals and actions, while a 2025 review describes goal-directed action mode as involving focused attention, action plans and continual feedback-driven updating. citeturn142070search13turn142070search2

Inhibitory control is also treated as a domain-general capacity for stopping or withholding action, with current neuroscience highlighting prefrontal mechanisms while recognizing that details remain under investigation. citeturn142070search4

## Computational hypothesis

TARA converts contextual appraisals into behavioral control signals:

`context -> appraisal -> valence/arousal/urgency/approach/inhibition/confidence`

The executive controller then performs:

`goals -> candidate actions -> value/cost/risk/confidence -> inhibition -> selection`

This is not a model of subjective emotion. The variables are control-state estimates.

## Why this belongs before tool use

The action layer must not simply execute the first action proposed by the language model. A separate executive controller can reject actions with excessive risk, unresolved conflict or required confirmation. This matches the broader computational idea that goal-directed behavior involves action evaluation plus flexible control. citeturn142070search6turn142070search15

## Validation

Initial tests check goal prioritization, action utility, risk inhibition, confirmation gating, appraisal response and regulation.

Future experiments should measure goal switching, conflict resolution, calibration under uncertainty, impulsive-action suppression and whether affective control signals improve socially appropriate responses without increasing hallucination or unnecessary refusal.
